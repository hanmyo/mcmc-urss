\begin{abstract}
Non-reversible parallel tempering replaces the random even--odd swap schedule of
classical parallel tempering with a deterministic alternation, turning a
replica's motion along the temperature ladder from a random walk into a
ballistic one. \citet{syed2022} show that the resulting round trip rate is
bounded away from zero as the ladder is refined, where the reversible rate decays
like $1/N$, and give a theory of the annealing schedule built on a single
quantity, the communication barrier $\Lambda$. We implement both schemes from
scratch and test that theory on an equal-weight mixture of two Gaussians in
$\R^d$ whose components have different widths, so that the two modes carry
identical probability while tempering distorts them against one another.

The predicted speed-up is real and quantitatively accurate: over a sixteen-fold
sweep in the number of chains, $\hat\Lambda$ is invariant to within $5\,\%$, the
non-reversible round trip rate rises to $93.8\,\%$ of its predicted ceiling
$(2+2\Lambda)^{-1}$, and at $200$ chains the non-reversible scheme completes $21$
round trips for every one the reversible scheme completes. Our main finding is
that this says remarkably little about the samples. Holding the algorithm, the
ladder, the dimension and the entire computational budget fixed and moving one
shape parameter, the communication barrier changes by $4\,\%$ and the predicted
round trip rate by $4\,\%$, while the recovered weight of one mode falls from
$0.508$ to $0.042$. Judged by its own diagnostic the sampler is in identical
health at both ends of that sweep. A Laplace argument explains why: tempering
flattens the energy barrier between modes but not the volume asymmetry between
them, and $\Lambda$ is assembled from swap acceptance rates, which are averages
over states the chains have actually visited. We show further that a mode-aware
statistic is not automatically safe either --- at $d=85$ six replicate chains
report a mean mode occupancy of exactly the correct value, $0.500$, without one
of them ever changing mode --- and we identify what does repair the failure: a
genuine reference distribution at $\beta=0$ in place of a truncated power path.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} Markov chain Monte Carlo; parallel tempering;
non-reversibility; multimodal sampling; communication barrier; convergence
diagnostics.


\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Aims and objectives}

The project set out to do three things.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Implement and verify.} Build both the reversible and the
  non-reversible schemes of \citet{syed2022} from scratch and check their
  quantitative predictions --- the $1/N$ decay of the reversible round trip rate,
  the $N$-independent limit of the non-reversible one, and the invariance of the
  communication barrier under refinement of the ladder --- on a target for which
  every quantity of interest is available in closed form.

  \item \textbf{Ask what the diagnostics measure.} The round trip rate is a
  statement about the index process on the temperature ladder. The quantity a
  practitioner cares about is whether the $\beta=1$ chain has visited both modes
  in the right proportion. These are different statements, and the project's
  central question is how tightly the first constrains the second.

  \item \textbf{Locate the mechanism.} Where the two come apart, identify what is
  responsible, separate genuine algorithmic limits from artefacts of tuning, and
  say which design choices repair the failure.
\end{enumerate}

A fourth objective --- extending the torpid-mixing conditions of
\citet{woodard2009torpid} from the reversible to the non-reversible setting ---
was scoped but not completed; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} explains the
obstruction and states the conjecture our numerics support.


\section{Background}

\subsection{Markov chain Monte Carlo}

Let $\pi$ be a density on $\R^d$ known only up to a normalising constant. Markov
chain Monte Carlo estimates $\E_\pi[f]$ by constructing a $\pi$-invariant kernel
$P$, running the chain it defines, and appealing to the ergodic theorem:
\begin{equation}
  \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n f(X_t)\;\longrightarrow\;\E_\pi[f]
  \qquad\text{almost surely,}
  \label{eq:ergodic}
\end{equation}
provided $P$ is $\pi$-irreducible and aperiodic. The standard construction is
Metropolis--Hastings: propose $y\sim q(\cdot\mid x)$ and accept with probability
\begin{equation}
  \alpha(x,y)\;=\;1\wedge\frac{\pi(y)q(x\mid y)}{\pi(x)q(y\mid x)} ,
  \label{eq:mh}
\end{equation}
which depends on $\pi$ only through ratios, so the normalising constant is never
needed. Throughout this report the local proposal is an isotropic Gaussian random
walk, for which $q$ is symmetric and \eqref{eq:mh} reduces to
$1\wedge\pi(y)/\pi(x)$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:ergodic} is an asymptotic statement and carries no rate. The
entire practical difficulty of MCMC is that the constant hidden in it can be
astronomically large, and the multimodal case is where this happens for reasons
that can be made completely explicit.

\subsection{Reversibility and detailed balance}

A kernel is \emph{reversible} with respect to $\pi$ if
\begin{equation}
  \pi(\mathrm dx)P(x,\mathrm dy)\;=\;\pi(\mathrm dy)P(y,\mathrm dx),
  \label{eq:db}
\end{equation}
that is, at stationarity the probability flux from $A$ to $B$ equals that from
$B$ to $A$. Detailed balance implies invariance and is much easier to verify,
which is why nearly every standard sampler is built to satisfy it.

It is sufficient, not necessary, and it has a cost. A reversible chain is
self-adjoint on $L^2(\pi)$ and its trajectories are statistically
indistinguishable from their time reversals, so it cannot sustain a net drift.
The consequence exploited in Section~\ref{sec:nrpt} is that to traverse a
one-dimensional sequence of $N$ states a reversible chain must diffuse, taking
$\Theta(N^2)$ steps, whereas a chain carrying an auxiliary direction can traverse
it ballistically in $\Theta(N)$.

\subsection{Multimodality and the failure of local samplers}

Suppose $\pi$ concentrates on two well-separated regions $A$ and $B$ with a
low-density region between them. A random walk Metropolis chain moves locally, so
to travel from $A$ to $B$ it must either make one enormous proposal, which the
proposal density makes vanishingly unlikely, or cross the gap in small steps each
accepted with probability of order $\pi(y)/\pi(x)\ll 1$.

The formal statement is via the conductance
\begin{equation}
  \Phi\;=\;\inf_{S:\ \pi(S)\le 1/2}
   \frac{\int_S P(x,S^c)\,\pi(\mathrm dx)}{\pi(S)},
  \label{eq:conductance}
\end{equation}
which by Cheeger-type inequalities bounds the spectral gap between $\Phi^2/2$ and
$2\Phi$. Taking $S=A$ makes $\Phi$ exponentially small in the barrier height and
hence, for product-form targets, in $d$. This is visible in every run reported
below: the control single chain never once changes mode, returning a mode
occupancy of exactly $0.00$ or $1.00$ against a truth of $0.500$, in every
configuration we tested.

\subsection{Tempering and the annealing path}

Tempering attacks the barrier rather than the chain, by interpolating between an
easy distribution and the target. The classical choice is the power path
\begin{equation}
  \pi_\beta(x)\;\propto\;\pi(x)^\beta,\qquad \beta\in(0,1],
  \label{eq:powerpath}
\end{equation}
which flattens $\pi$ as $\beta$ falls, raising the trough between the modes and
improving \eqref{eq:conductance}. \citet{syed2022} work with the more general
\emph{annealing path}
\begin{equation}
  \pi_\beta(x)\;\propto\;\pi(x)^{\beta}\,\pi_0(x)^{1-\beta},
  \label{eq:refpath}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_0$ is a normalised reference one can sample from directly. The
distinction matters more than it appears. Under \eqref{eq:powerpath} the limit
$\beta\to0$ is improper, so one truncates at some $\beta_{\min}>0$ and the
hottest chain is still a flattened copy of the target --- with all of the
target's asymmetries intact. Under \eqref{eq:refpath} the chain at $\beta=0$
\emph{is} $\pi_0$, mixes by construction and supplies genuinely independent
restarts. Section~\ref{sec:results-repair} shows this to be the single most
consequential design choice in the study, and it is not a choice the theory of
Section~\ref{sec:nrpt} makes for you.

\subsection{Reversible parallel tempering}

Parallel tempering discretises the path at
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal P_N:\quad 1=\beta_0>\beta_1>\dots>\beta_{N-1}=\beta_{\min}\ge0,
\end{equation}
and runs one chain per rung on $\R^{d\times N}$ with invariant density
$\bigotimes_j\pi_{\beta_j}$. Chain $0$ targets $\pi$ and supplies the samples we
keep; the rest exist to feed it mobility. We call chain $0$ the \emph{target}
chain and chain $N-1$ the \emph{reference} chain.\footnote{Some diagnostic
figures produced by our code index the ladder in the opposite direction, with $0$
the reference; the axis labels state which convention is in force.}

A \emph{scan} is two moves. In the \emph{exploration} move each chain
independently performs $T$ random walk Metropolis steps against its own
$\pi_{\beta_j}$; these are embarrassingly parallel. In the \emph{swap} move
neighbouring chains exchange states, the pair $(j,j+1)$ being accepted with
probability $1\wedge A_j$ where
\begin{equation}
  A_j=\frac{\pi_{\beta_j}(x_{j+1})\pi_{\beta_{j+1}}(x_j)}
           {\pi_{\beta_j}(x_j)\pi_{\beta_{j+1}}(x_{j+1})}
     =\exp\!\big\{(\beta_{j+1}-\beta_j)[\log\pi(x_j)-\log\pi(x_{j+1})]\big\},
  \label{eq:swapratio}
\end{equation}
the second equality holding on the power path. Adjacent pairs overlap, so one
swaps either the even-indexed pairs or the odd ones. In \emph{reversible}, or
\emph{stochastic even--odd} (SEO), parallel tempering the parity is a fresh
$\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ draw each scan; the resulting kernel satisfies
\eqref{eq:db}, and it is exactly this randomisation that makes a replica's
position on the ladder a random walk.


\section{Non-reversible parallel tempering}
\label{sec:nrpt}

\subsection{The deterministic even--odd swap scheme}

The non-reversible scheme changes one line: at scan $i$, swap the even pairs if
$i$ is even and the odd pairs if $i$ is odd. The ladder, the exploration kernel
and the acceptance ratio \eqref{eq:swapratio} are untouched. We call this
\emph{deterministic even--odd} (DEO).

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{One parallel tempering scan. SEO and DEO differ only in line~6.}
\label{alg:pt}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \State \textbf{input:} ladder $\beta$, states $x_0,\dots,x_{N-1}$, scan index $i$
  \For{$j=0,\dots,N-1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \For{$t=1,\dots,T$}
      \State $y\sim\mathcal N(x_j,\sigma_j^2 I)$
      \State $x_j\leftarrow y$ with probability
             $1\wedge\pi_{\beta_j}(y)/\pi_{\beta_j}(x_j)$
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
  \State $p\leftarrow\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ \ \textbf{(SEO)}
         \qquad $p\leftarrow i\bmod 2$ \ \textbf{(DEO)}
  \For{$j=p,\,p+2,\dots\le N-2$}
    \State swap $x_j\leftrightarrow x_{j+1}$ with probability $1\wedge A_j$
  \EndFor
  \State record the chain index occupied by each replica
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The parity is now a deterministic function of the scan counter, so the swap
kernel no longer satisfies \eqref{eq:db}. Invariance of the product measure
survives, because each individual swap remains a reversible move and invariance
is closed under composition. What is gained is momentum: a replica just passed
from chain $j$ to chain $j+1$ by an even sweep is offered the pair $(j+1,j+2)$ on
the following odd sweep --- a move onward rather than a move back. With perfect
swap acceptance a replica crosses the whole ladder in $N$ scans against
$\Theta(N^2)$ for SEO.

\subsection{Round trip rate}

Let $\mathcal I_i^{\,r}$ denote the chain occupied by replica $r$ at the end of
scan $i$; the vector process $\mathcal I_i$ is the \emph{index process}. A
\emph{round trip} is a completed excursion of one replica from the target chain
to the reference chain and back, and the \emph{round trip rate} is
\begin{equation}
  \tau=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\E[T_n]}{n},
  \label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
$T_n$ being the number of round trips completed by all replicas in $n$ scans.
It is the natural efficiency measure because each round trip delivers to the
target chain one state that has travelled to the reference and back --- one
approximately independent restart.

It is worth being precise about what \eqref{eq:tau} is a statement about. It is a
functional of $\mathcal I$, a process on $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$. It refers to the
target $\pi$ only through the swap acceptance rates that drive $\mathcal I$. This
observation is trivial as stated and is the pivot of the entire report.

\begin{corollary}[\citealt{syed2022}, Corollary~1]
\label{cor:syed1}
For any annealing schedule $\mathcal P_N$,
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\mathrm{SEO}}(\mathcal P_N)=\frac{1}{2N+2E(\mathcal P_N)},
  \qquad
  \tau_{\mathrm{DEO}}(\mathcal P_N)=\frac{1}{2+2E(\mathcal P_N)},
  \label{eq:cor1}
\end{equation}
where the \emph{schedule inefficiency} is
$E(\mathcal P_N)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2} r_j/s_j$, with $s_j$ the swap acceptance rate
for the pair $(j,j+1)$ and $r_j=1-s_j$.
\end{corollary}

The structural content is the placement of $N$: in the denominator for SEO, so
refining the ladder costs and $\tau_{\mathrm{SEO}}\to0$ like $1/N$; absent for
DEO, so refining is asymptotically free. This is the whole practical case for
non-reversibility.

\subsection{The communication barrier}

The rejection rates are not free parameters. They aggregate into a quantity
belonging to the path rather than to its discretisation.

\begin{definition}[Communication barrier]
\label{def:barrier}
$\displaystyle \hat\Lambda(\mathcal P_N)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}r_j
 =\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}(1-s_j)$.
\end{definition}

\citet{syed2022} show that as the mesh $\|\mathcal P_N\|\to0$,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\Lambda(\mathcal P_N)=\Lambda+O\!\big(N\|\mathcal P_N\|^3\big),
  \qquad
  \Lambda=\int_{\beta_{\min}}^{1}\lambda(\beta)\,\mathrm d\beta,
  \label{eq:barrierlimit}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda$ the local communication barrier, an explicit functional of the
path. So $\Lambda$ is invariant under refinement: adding chains changes each
$r_j$ but not their sum. Since $E\ge\hat\Lambda$, with equality in the limit,
\eqref{eq:cor1} gives

\begin{theorem}[\citealt{syed2022}, Theorem~3]
\label{thm:syed3}
Along a sequence of refining schedules,
$\tau_{\mathrm{SEO}}\to0$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{DEO}}\to\bar\tau=(2+2\Lambda)^{-1}$.
\end{theorem}

Because $\tau_{\mathrm{DEO}}$ rises towards $\bar\tau$ while the cost per scan
grows linearly in $N$, round trips per unit of computation are maximised at a
finite chain count, and \citet{syed2022} obtain $N^\ast\approx2\Lambda$. The
optimal placement of rungs equalises the local barrier across pairs, which in
practice means estimating $\lambda$ from a pilot run and inverting its cumulative
integral.

\subsection{What these quantities can and cannot see}
\label{sec:whatcansee}

It is worth stating plainly, before any experiment, what the theory of this
section is a theory of.

$\Lambda$, $E$ and $\tau$ are built from the swap acceptance rates $s_j$, and
each $s_j$ is an expectation of \eqref{eq:swapratio} under the joint law of the
two chains it connects. An expectation is taken over the states the chains have
actually visited. If some region $A\subset\R^d$ is never reached by any chain on
the ladder, no $s_j$ is affected by it, and therefore neither $\hat\Lambda$ nor
$\hat\tau$ can report that anything is wrong. The diagnostics are estimated from
the sampler's own trajectory, and a sampler cannot use its own trajectory to
detect what its trajectory has omitted.

None of this is a defect of \citet{syed2022}, whose results are stated for the
index process and claim nothing about the geometry of $\pi$. It is a statement
about how the results are used. A healthy round trip rate is necessary for good
sampling; whether it is anywhere close to sufficient is an empirical question,
and it is the question this report answers. The target of
Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} is designed so that the answer is unambiguous: we can
move the difficulty of the sampling problem through several orders of magnitude
while holding $\Lambda$ fixed, and watch what the diagnostics do.


\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Implementation}

Both schemes were implemented from scratch in Python with NumPy, vectorised
across chains so that a scan is a handful of array operations rather than a loop
over $N$. SEO and DEO share the entire code path and differ only in the line that
sets the swap parity, so any difference between them is attributable to the
schedule. For every configuration a control single-chain random walk Metropolis
sampler runs at $\beta=1$ on the same total budget of density evaluations,
supplying the ``no tempering'' baseline.

\paragraph{Log-space evaluation.}
All densities and acceptance ratios are computed in log space via log-sum-exp.
This is a correctness requirement, not a refinement. Forming \eqref{eq:swapratio}
from raw densities underflows at moderate $d$: at $d=30$ both $\pi(x)$ and
$\pi(y)$ evaluate to exactly zero for $\beta\lesssim0.02$, the ratio becomes
$0/0$, and the hottest $\sim\!15\,\%$ of the ladder silently accepts every
proposal. An early version of our code had that bug, and its symptom --- swap
rates of $1.000$ at the hot end together with a small $\hat\Lambda$ --- reads as
success rather than as failure. It is worth flagging because it is a second
instance of the report's theme: a diagnostic looking healthy for the wrong
reason.

\subsection{Benchmark targets}
\label{sec:benchmark}

The target throughout is an equal-weight mixture of two isotropic Gaussians with
centres at $\pm\mathbf1_d$ and \emph{different} widths,
\begin{equation}
  \pi(x)=\tfrac12\,\mathcal N\!\big(x;+\mathbf1_d,\,s_1^2I\big)
        +\tfrac12\,\mathcal N\!\big(x;-\mathbf1_d,\,s_2^2I\big),
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
with $A=\{x:x_1>c\}$ the basin of the $+\mathbf1_d$ mode, $c=1-2s_1/(s_1+s_2)$
the variance-weighted midpoint, and $B$ its complement.

This construction is the instrument of the whole report, and it was chosen for
three properties. Both modes carry probability exactly $\tfrac12$, so the correct
mode-$A$ occupancy is $0.500$ for every $d$, $s_1$ and $s_2$ --- the right answer
never moves as we sweep. The separation is set by $d$. And the inhomogeneity is
set by the single ratio $s_2/s_1$, so we can make the sampling problem
arbitrarily hard \emph{without changing the answer and without changing anything
the theory of Section~\ref{sec:nrpt} looks at}. Setting $s_1=s_2$ recovers the
symmetric benchmark for which \citet{woodard2009rapid} prove rapid mixing, so a
sweep in $s_2/s_1$ interpolates from a case known to be easy to one that
\citet{woodard2009torpid} show is torpid.

Why inhomogeneity should matter is visible from a Laplace approximation. On the
power path \eqref{eq:powerpath}, near mode $i$ the tempered density
$\pi^\beta$ is Gaussian with variance $s_i^2/\beta$ and total mass proportional
to $(s_i^2)^{-d\beta/2}(s_i^2/\beta)^{d/2}$. The $\beta$ factors cancel in the
ratio, leaving
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \frac{\pi_\beta(A)}{\pi_\beta(B)}
   =\left(\frac{s_1}{s_2}\right)^{d(1-\beta)}. \ }
  \label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
The modes are balanced only at $\beta=1$, and the imbalance is exponential in
$d(1-\beta)$. Tempering flattens the \emph{energy} barrier between the modes; it
does nothing to the \emph{volume} asymmetry between them, and in fact makes it
worse, monotonically, all the way up the ladder. At the $d=30$ benchmark with
$s_1=0.3$, $s_2=0.4$ and $\beta_{\min}=0.01$, \eqref{eq:laplace} gives
$1.9\times10^{-4}$: the reference chain --- the one chain free to move between
modes --- is itself $5000$ to $1$ against mode $A$, so almost every replica that
descends the ladder carries mode $B$ back down with it. Round trips are necessary
for mode mixing; \eqref{eq:laplace} is the reason they are not sufficient. We
write $D=d\lvert\log(s_2/s_1)\rvert$, in nats, for the resulting
\emph{mode-weight distortion}.

\subsection{Performance measures}

Five quantities are recorded per run: the per-chain local acceptance rate,
compared with the optimal $0.234$ of \citet{roberts1997}; the swap acceptance
rates and the barrier $\hat\Lambda$ of Definition~\ref{def:barrier}, estimated
separately under SEO and DEO as an internal consistency check; the round trip
rate $\tau$ of \eqref{eq:tau}, compared with Corollary~\ref{cor:syed1} evaluated
at the measured $s_j$; the \emph{mode-$A$ occupancy}, the proportion of retained
$\beta=1$ samples in $A$, whose truth is $0.500$; and $\hat R$, the
Gelman--Rubin statistic computed \emph{across replicate runs started alternately
in the two modes}.

The first three describe the ladder, the fourth describes the samples, and the
fifth describes the agreement between independent samplers. The distance between
the first three and the fourth is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:results-blind};
the distance between the fourth and the fifth is the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:results-repair}.

\subsection{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}

Two campaigns were run, differing in the annealing path, the proposal scaling
and the replication.

\paragraph{Campaign I: power path, fixed proposal radius.}
Path \eqref{eq:powerpath}; $\beta$ geometric on $[0.01,1]$; proposal standard
deviation $0.15/\sqrt\beta$, \emph{not} scaled with $d$; $50\,000$ scans of $64$
exploration moves per chain; one run per configuration; every chain initialised
at $x=-\mathbf1_d$, i.e.\ in mode $B$; no burn-in discarded. Three sweeps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(I.a)] \emph{Chain count.} $d=30$, $s_1=0.3$, $s_2=0.4$,
  $N\in\{25,50,100,200,400\}$.
  \item[(I.b)] \emph{Dimension.} $N=60$, $s_1=0.3$, $s_2=0.4$,
  $d\in\{5,10,20,40,80,160\}$.
  \item[(I.c)] \emph{Inhomogeneity.} $d=25$, $N=65$, $s_1=0.20$ fixed,
  $s_2\in\{0.14,0.15,0.16,0.18,0.20\}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Campaign II: reference path, scaled proposals, replicated.}
Path \eqref{eq:refpath} with an explicit reference $\pi_0$, so $\beta_0=0$ is a
distribution one can sample directly; ladder
$\{0\}\cup\mathrm{geomspace}(10^{-3},1)$ over $N=48$ chains, optionally retuned to
equalise the local barrier; proposal standard deviation
$2.38\,\sigma_{\mathrm{path}}(\beta)/\sqrt d$ after \citet{roberts1997}, with
$\sigma_{\mathrm{path}}(\beta)^2=[(1-\beta)/\sigma_0^2+2\beta/(s_1^2+s_2^2)]^{-1}$
the width of $\pi_\beta$; $s_1=0.2$, $s_2=0.3$; six replicate runs per
configuration started alternately in the two modes, with $\hat R$ reported. Two
references were used: an uninformative $\pi_0=\mathcal N(0,\sigma_0^2I)$ with
$\sigma_0=1$, and a moment-matched mixture fitted to the target. The sweep was in
dimension, $d\in\{15,20,25,30,35,45,50,75,85,100,200\}$.

All runs used a single CPU core, with wall times of $100$--$780\,\mathrm{s}$ per
configuration in Campaign~I and about $200$--$310\,\mathrm{s}$ in Campaign~II.


\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The speed-up is real}
\label{sec:results-speedup}

At the Campaign~I benchmark ($d=30$, $s_1=0.3$, $s_2=0.4$, $N=100$) the two
schemes recover the same communication barrier to three significant figures,
$\hat\Lambda=10.07$ under both, as Definition~\ref{def:barrier} requires: the
barrier belongs to the path and the two schemes share a path. Their per-chain
acceptance rates agree to $0.001$. Every difference between them is therefore
attributable to the swap sequencing alone --- which is the point of implementing
them on a shared code path.

They differ exactly as Corollary~\ref{cor:syed1} predicts. The measured rates are
$\tau_{\mathrm{SEO}}=3.7\times10^{-3}$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{DEO}}=38.4\times10^{-3}$, a factor of $10.4$ against a predicted
$9.1$. The index process shows the mechanism directly: under DEO a replica sweeps
the ladder end to end in roughly $N$ scans, while under SEO it diffuses near the
cold end and reaches the reference chain only occasionally. Nothing in what
follows is a challenge to this result. The non-reversible scheme does what
\citet{syed2022} say it does, and does it accurately.

All three structural predictions hold. \textbf{The barrier is invariant in $N$}:
across a sixteen-fold increase in the chain count $\hat\Lambda$ moves from
$9.54$ to $10.04$, a spread of $5\,\%$, while the mean swap rate climbs from
$0.602$ to $0.975$. The individual rejection rates fall by a factor of sixteen
and their sum does not move --- the content of \eqref{eq:barrierlimit}, with the
residual drift consistent with the $O(N\|\mathcal P_N\|^3)$ error.
\textbf{$\tau_{\mathrm{SEO}}$ decays like $1/N$ while $\tau_{\mathrm{DEO}}$
saturates}: taking $\Lambda\approx10.04$ gives $\bar\tau=45.3\times10^{-3}$, and
the measured DEO rate climbs monotonically through $57$, $76$, $85$, $89$ and
$94$ per cent of that ceiling while SEO falls by a factor of six between $N=25$ and $N=200$ --- slightly
faster than $1/N$, because the schedule inefficiency $E$ falls from $15.8$ to
$10.3$ as the ladder refines, so both terms in the denominator of
\eqref{eq:cor1} move the same way.
\textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:syed1} is quantitatively accurate}: measured DEO rates
are $87$--$96\,\%$ of prediction at every chain count, and SEO rates
$79$--$94\,\%$ excluding the censored entry. The systematic shortfall has two
identifiable sources --- the theory is stationary and $50\,000$ scans is finite,
and the prediction assumes full equilibration between swaps whereas ours performs
$64$ moves.

The $N=400$ row should not be quoted as a $142\times$ speed-up: SEO completed
about $15$ round trips in the entire run there, so its estimate is dominated by
its own discretisation, and Corollary~\ref{cor:syed1} predicts $37\times$. The
defensible headline is $N=200$: \textbf{DEO completes $21$ round trips for every
one SEO completes}, against a predicted $18$.

One qualification frames everything that follows. For this target
$N^\ast\approx2\Lambda\approx20$, so every row of Table~\ref{tab:chains} sits at
or beyond the compute-optimal chain count. The table measures the robustness of
DEO to over-refinement --- the regime where its advantage is largest by
construction --- rather than performance at a well-chosen $N$.

closely related experiment that does test it.

\paragraph{The reference distribution is what repairs the occupancy.}
Under the truncated power path, occupancy collapsed to $0.002$ somewhere between
$d=20$ and $d=40$. With a genuine reference and a scaled proposal it is within
$5\,\%$ of the truth at every $d$ up to $75$, where the distortion is $D=30.4$
nats --- a regime in which \eqref{eq:laplace} on a power path would give a weight
ratio of $6\times10^{-14}$. It degrades only at $d\ge85$, by $23\,\%$ there and
$26\,\%$ at $d=100$. The reason is structural. On the path
\eqref{eq:refpath} the distortion is non-monotone in $\beta$ and vanishes at
\emph{both} endpoints, because $\beta=0$ is the reference and the reference does
not distinguish the modes. Repeating the Laplace calculation on this path gives a
worst-case weight ratio of $\exp(-0.307\,d)$ attained at an interior
$\beta^\ast\approx0.13$ independent of $d$. The bottleneck moves from the top of
the ladder to its middle, and a replica that reaches $\beta=0$ genuinely
restarts. Section~\ref{sec:results-blind} is therefore a result about the power
path with a truncated ladder, not about parallel tempering as such --- an
important qualification, and one that strengthens rather than weakens the main
point, because nothing in $\hat\Lambda$ or $\tau$ distinguished the two paths.

\paragraph{A mode-aware statistic is not automatically safe either.}
At $d=85$ with an uninformative $\mathcal N(0,I)$ reference, the six replicates
report a mean mode-$A$ occupancy of exactly $0.500$ --- the correct answer to
three decimal places --- while $\hat R=77$ for SEO and $110$ for DEO. The
per-run marginals resolve it: the three runs started in mode $A$ report occupancy
$1.00$, and the three started in mode $B$ report $0.00$. Not one of the six
chains ever changed mode. The exactly correct answer is an artefact of averaging
six maximally wrong ones, and the only statistic that caught it was $\hat R$
across replicates deliberately started in different modes. The same configuration
with a moment-matched mixture reference gives per-run occupancies of $0.61$ and
$0.23$ under DEO with $\hat R=1.05$: a sampler that is genuinely mixing, whose
diagnostics say so, and whose answer is correspondingly imperfect.

\paragraph{Non-reversibility buys some sample quality, but far less than it buys
round trips.} At $d=85$ DEO's per-run occupancies ($0.61$, $0.23$) straddle the
truth more tightly than SEO's ($0.71$, $0.13$), and its $\hat R$ is lower
($1.05$ against $1.19$). In Table~\ref{tab:occ}, at $N\ge100$ DEO is wrong by a factor of $2$--$5$ where
SEO is wrong by up to $250$; at $N=25$ and $50$ neither scheme reaches mode $A$
at all, so non-reversibility buys nothing there. So the non-reversible scheme is
better on sample quality as well --- but by a factor of a few, in a setting where
it is better on round trips by a factor of $21$. The two speed-ups are not the
same number, and reporting the second in place of the first overstates the case
by an order of magnitude.


\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Two findings should be separated, because the second is easily mistaken for a
criticism of the first.

The first is a confirmation. On a target where every quantity is known in closed
form, the theory of \citet{syed2022} predicts what a from-scratch implementation
does: the communication barrier is invariant under refinement to $5\,\%$, the
non-reversible round trip rate rises to $94\,\%$ of its predicted ceiling while
the reversible rate decays like $1/N$, and Corollary~1 is accurate to
$87$--$96\,\%$ wherever its hypotheses hold. The two departures are both traceable
to hypotheses failing rather than conclusions failing --- a censored estimator at
$N=400$, and a proposal radius that does not scale with $d$ above $d=40$ --- and
both were removed in Campaign~II with the expected effect. The square-root growth
of the barrier, reproduced on two different paths, is a genuinely encouraging
result: the chain count required grows like $\sqrt d$, not like $d$.

The second is that these quantities are a poor proxy for sample quality, and the
gap is not small. Table~\ref{tab:sigma} is the cleanest statement of it: across a
series in which the sampler goes from recovering the mode weights to missing them
by a factor of twelve, $\hat\Lambda$ moves by $4\,\%$ and the predicted round trip
rate by $4\,\%$. This is not a flaw in \citet{syed2022}, whose results are stated
for the index process and claim nothing about the geometry of $\pi$. It is a
statement about inference from diagnostics. $\Lambda$, $E$ and $\tau$ are
estimated from swap acceptance rates, which are averages over states the chains
have visited, and no such statistic can report on a region the sampler has never
reached. Tempering removes the energy barrier between modes; it does not remove
the volume asymmetry \eqref{eq:laplace} that starves the ladder of one of them.

Section~\ref{sec:results-repair} sharpens this in two directions at once. The
failure is not intrinsic to parallel tempering --- replacing a truncated power
path by a genuine reference distribution recovers correct occupancies out to
$d=75$ at a distortion of $30$ nats --- but the diagnostics did not distinguish
the working design from the broken one. And a target-referring statistic is not
sufficient on its own: the $d=85$ run in which six chains report a mean occupancy
of exactly $0.500$ without one of them ever changing mode is a case where the
sample-quality statistic was exactly right for exactly the wrong reason. The
practical recommendation is therefore a three-layer one: report $\tau$ and
$\hat\Lambda$ to diagnose the ladder, an occupancy or other target-referring
statistic to diagnose the samples, and a cross-replicate $\hat R$ from
deliberately dispersed starting points to diagnose the occupancy.

\paragraph{Relation to the torpid mixing literature.}
\citet{woodard2009rapid} give sufficient conditions for rapid mixing of parallel
and simulated tempering on multimodal targets, and \citet{woodard2009torpid}
sufficient conditions for torpid mixing. The relevant contrast is that the
rapid-mixing results apply when the target is symmetric with respect to the
partition into modes, while torpid mixing can be forced by letting the modes
differ in shape --- precisely the $s_1\ne s_2$ construction of
\eqref{eq:target}. Their mechanism, a set whose persistence across temperatures
decays exponentially, is the measure-theoretic form of what \eqref{eq:laplace}
computes here, and our results are consistent with theirs: occupancy fails in
exactly the configurations where the distortion $D$ is large and the path does
not remove it.

Extending those conditions to the non-reversible case was an objective of this
project and was not achieved. The obstruction is structural rather than
technical: the Woodard--Schmidler--Huber bounds are spectral, and their
decomposition argument uses reversibility twice --- once to make the swapping
chain self-adjoint so that the spectral gap controls the mixing time, and once in
the state decomposition itself. A DEO chain is not self-adjoint and its spectral
gap does not directly control its mixing time, so the statement must be
reformulated before it can be proved. What the numerics establish is that the
answer is unlikely to be qualitatively different. In every configuration where
SEO fails to recover the mode weights, DEO fails too --- sometimes by a smaller
factor (two to five) and sometimes just as badly; non-reversibility accelerates the index
process, and the index process is not what is broken. The natural conjecture is
that the torpid-mixing conditions carry over with the $O(N^2)$ ladder-traversal
factor replaced by $O(N)$ and the exponential-in-$d$ factor untouched ---
consistent with the whole thrust of this report, in which non-reversibility
improves the ladder dynamics by a large constant and leaves the geometry of $\pi$
exactly where it found it. Establishing it would most plausibly go through the
piecewise-deterministic Markov process limit of \citet{syed2022} rather than
through a spectral argument.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{(i)} Campaign~I used one run per configuration. The round trip rate is
stable under replication --- two independent $d=40$ runs agree to $2.5\,\%$
($0.0288$ against $0.0281$) --- but the occupancy is not: those same two runs give
$0.464$ and $0.002$ under SEO. Mode-weight estimates there are dominated by rare
events and a single $50\,000$-scan run cannot pin them down, which is why
Campaign~II replicates six times and reports $\hat R$. \emph{(ii)} No burn-in is
discarded and every Campaign~I chain starts in mode $B$, so occupancy is biased
towards $B$ by construction; the $d\le20$ runs return $0.42$--$0.52$, showing the
bias is small when the sampler mixes, but it inflates the apparent failure in the
slow runs. \emph{(iii)} Sweep~(I.c) is at $d=25$ with $65$ chains, not at the
$d=30$ benchmark, so it should be read within itself. \emph{(iv)} The benchmark is
a two-component Gaussian mixture with isotropic components, chosen for
tractability; nothing here establishes that the conclusions transfer to curved or
strongly anisotropic modes.


\section{Conclusion}

\textbf{Non-reversibility buys an order of magnitude and more.} With the target
fixed at $d=30$, the DEO round trip rate rose to $93.8\,\%$ of its $N\to\infty$
ceiling $\bar\tau=(2+2\Lambda)^{-1}$ while the SEO rate fell like $1/N$; at $200$
chains DEO completed $21$ round trips for every one SEO completed, against a
predicted $18$.

\textbf{The theory is accurate wherever its assumptions hold.} Measured rates sat
at $87$--$96\,\%$ of Corollary~1 for every chain count and for every $d\le40$.
The two gaps that appear are both explained: SEO at $400$ chains is censored by
the run length, and above $d=40$ a proposal radius that does not scale with
dimension breaks the local-exploration assumption before the theory breaks.

\textbf{The communication barrier scales like $\sqrt d$.} Two campaigns with
different annealing paths, mode widths and chain counts give
$\hat\Lambda=1.81\,d^{0.497}$ and $\hat\Lambda=2.02\,d^{0.474}$, each clean to a
few percent, so the chain count needed to keep the ladder connected grows only
like $\sqrt d$.

\textbf{Round trip rate is not a diagnostic of sample quality.} In every failing
run the barrier, the swap rates and the predicted round trip rate all looked
healthy while the mode weights were wrong by factors of two to $250$. In the
controlled sweep of Table~\ref{tab:sigma}, $\hat\Lambda$ moved by $4\,\%$ and the
predicted $\tau$ by $4\,\%$ while the recovered mode weight fell by $92\,\%$.
Tempering removes the energy barrier between modes; it does not remove the volume
asymmetry $(s_1/s_2)^{d(1-\beta)}$ that starves the ladder of one of them, and
$\tau$ is blind to the difference.

\textbf{Nor is an occupancy, on its own.} At $d=85$ six replicate chains reported
a mean mode occupancy of exactly $0.500$ --- the correct answer --- without one of
them ever changing mode, and only a cross-replicate $\hat R$ from dispersed
starts revealed it. Report all three layers: $\tau$ and $\hat\Lambda$ for the
ladder, a target-referring statistic for the samples, and $\hat R$ for the
target-referring statistic.

\textbf{Next.} Choose $\beta_{\min}$ and the reference distribution from the
target rather than fixing them --- most of what looks like algorithmic failure at
moderate $d$ turns out to be a failure of path design that the theory is not
intended to catch --- and formalise the extension of the torpid-mixing conditions
of \citet{woodard2009torpid} to the non-reversible case, for which the evidence
here gives a clear conjecture and the piecewise-deterministic limit of
\citet{syed2022} the most likely route.
